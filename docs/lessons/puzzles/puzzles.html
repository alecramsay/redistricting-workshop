Redistricting Puzzles

This applet lets you explore how district lines control the translation of votes into seats. You'll draw 4 maps for a simple state with 64 voters — 32 who vote Red (R) and 32 who vote Blue (B) — arranged in an 8×8 grid.

When you draw a map:

  • Each district must have exactly 16 voters.
  • A district is won by Red if it has 9 or more Red voters; by Blue if it has 9 or more Blue voters. A district with exactly 8 Red and 8 Blue voters is a tie, i.e., perfectly competitive.
  • Districts must be contiguous: every voter in a district must share a "fence" with at least one neighboring voter in the same district.
  • The letter inside each square shows the voter's party (Red or Blue).
  • The default partisan lean view mode paints the background color to show who is winning the district: red, blue, or yellow for a tie. The map color option shows the district colors instead.
  • Click a district row in the panel to select it, then click voters on the map to assign them to that district. Clicking a voter already in the active district removes it from that district.

There are 4 puzzles in the dropdown:

  • Competitive — Draw 4 districts that are each exactly tied.
  • Sweetheart — Draw 2 safe Red districts and 2 safe Blue districts.
  • Packing — Pack Red voters into 1 district, so Blue wins the other 3.
  • Cracking — Crack Blue voters across 3 Red-winning districts, so Blue only wins 1.
